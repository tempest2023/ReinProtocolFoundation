## Design Context

### Users

Rein serves a mixed global community of students, researchers, engineers, educators, organizers, institutional partners, and public-interest participants. Visitors should be able to understand the Mission, meet the people behind the work, find free learning and public gatherings, and choose an appropriate level of participation without already knowing nonprofit or AI-community terminology. Administrators need a calm, traceable workspace for publishing, reviewing applications, and recovering from automation failures.

### Brand Personality

Credible, human, and mission-driven. The voice is precise about legal status and institutional relationships, generous toward newcomers, and candid about work that is still forming. It should feel like a thoughtful public institution with real people in it—not a startup growth funnel, exclusive club, speculative token project, or generic AI product.

### Aesthetic Direction

Retain the existing light, warm-paper palette, editorial Newsreader typography, restrained international-orange accent, fine rules, spacious composition, documentary imagery, and public-institution character. Community pages may introduce warmer portraits, gatherings, and more conversational copy while remaining visually continuous with the institutional site. Prefer asymmetrical editorial compositions over repetitive card grids. Avoid glossy gradients, excessive pills, dashboard chrome on public pages, decorative motion, and fabricated social proof.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with the Mission and the public value; treat growth metrics as transparent context, not proof of activity or legal membership.
2. Make low-pressure participation obvious: most resources and public events require no registration, and Contributor applications are only for deeper responsibility.
3. Show people and work separately so the community's human network remains visible without turning profiles into status badges.
4. Keep launch-state implementation details out of public copy. Empty sections may describe their purpose, but never announce that content or backend services are unfinished. Report temporary submission failures only after the user attempts to submit.
5. Design for global names, regions, scripts, long text, keyboard use, reduced motion, and recoverable errors from the first release.
6. In administration, expose provenance, status, evidence, consent, and manual recovery before visual density or automation convenience.
7. Use the shared semantic type tokens for page titles, page leads, primary section titles, and body copy; never introduce route-specific sizes for the same typographic role.
8. Give each primary institutional page its own source-appropriate hero artwork; do not reuse another page’s hero image as a placeholder.
