# 1. Purpose

Rein Protocol Foundation exists to guide, accelerate and steward the development of AI Agents for the benefit of humanity.

Its mission is both constructive and protective:

* to help AI Agents create measurable public benefit;
* to direct intelligence, capital and technical capacity toward human flourishing;
* to develop institutions and safeguards that reduce catastrophic and existential risks from increasingly capable AI systems, including the risk that AGI could severely damage or destroy human civilization;
* to demonstrate forms of Agent-operated organization that remain aligned with humanity's long-term interests.

Rein Protocol may pursue this mission through research, public education, open technology, standards, grants, direct programs, safety mechanisms, governance experiments and public-benefit infrastructure.

## Confirmed Public Mission

> **Rein Protocol Foundation advances the development of AI Agents that benefit human society and works to prevent worst-case outcomes in an AGI/ASI era, including catastrophic damage to humanity, its institutions, its forms of social organization and its ability to govern its own future.**

The mission is not limited to technical alignment or extinction prevention. It includes preserving human agency, social continuity, institutional capacity, public safety and the ability of human communities to choose how increasingly capable AI systems participate in society.

The public message should use a two-layer structure:

1. **Constructive:** promote AI Agents that create practical benefit for human society;
2. **Protective:** prevent AGI, ASI and autonomous Agent systems from causing catastrophic or irreversible harm to humanity and human social organization.

## Initial Charitable Program Portfolio

The initial program portfolio is confirmed as follows.

### 1. Public Voice and AI Community Education

Build public communication channels in AI communities to explain the Foundation's mission, encourage informed discussion of beneficial and safe AI Agents and develop a credible community capable of participating in that work.

Increasing the Foundation's visibility and community influence is a means of advancing public education, participation and mission delivery; it is not an independent charitable end. Communications and fundraising costs must be classified separately from educational program expenses where accounting rules require it.

Initial activities may include:

- accessible articles, interviews, podcasts, newsletters and public research summaries;
- online discussions and community forums;
- multilingual public-education material;
- collaboration with researchers, builders, safety practitioners and civil-society organizations.

### 2. AI Agent Research and Conference Events

Follow and contribute to AI Agent, ethics, safety and infrastructure research. Organize workshops, panels, meetups and other educational events associated with or held alongside major research conferences such as ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR and ACL.

An event may be described as an official conference workshop, tutorial or affiliated event only after acceptance or written authorization from the relevant conference. Otherwise, it must be described accurately as an independent or community-organized event held during the same period or in the same city.

Priority subjects may include:

- AI Agent ethics and governance;
- technical and social safety infrastructure;
- oversight, evaluation and controllability of autonomous systems;
- Agent effects on institutions, labor, economic coordination and collective decision-making;
- public-benefit applications and open standards for beneficial Agents.

### 3. Free AI, LLM and AI Agent Education

Create free and openly accessible courses that help more people learn AI, LLM and AI Agent concepts and encourage participation in relevant research and industry.

Programs may include:

- introductory and advanced online courses;
- open curricula, code, exercises and recorded lectures;
- research-reading groups and project-based learning;
- mentorship, office hours and pathways into research or responsible industry work.

Course content should be educational rather than promotional, remain reasonably vendor-neutral and be published under open licenses where practicable.

### 4. Learning Communities and Public Events

Build online communities and local learning groups around the courses. Recruit learners and community members and organize primarily free AI events addressing AI Agent safety, infrastructure, ethics, research and responsible deployment.

Programs should be open to a sufficiently broad charitable class rather than operated as a private club. If a fee is ever necessary for cost recovery, the Foundation should provide waivers or free access where practicable and must not make ability to pay the principal basis for educational access.

Community membership does not by itself create ownership of Foundation assets, a right to future Tokens or a charitable tax deduction. Governance rights, if any, must be separately defined in the Bylaws and DAO constitution.

## Initial Use of Funds

Initial donations may be used for the four program areas above and the reasonable organizational infrastructure needed to deliver them, including:

- educational content and curriculum production;
- research, publication and open-source work;
- conference, workshop, venue and community-event costs;
- accessibility, translation and participant support;
- website, community and technical infrastructure;
- qualified personnel, instructors, contractors and operational AI systems;
- legal, accounting, security, compliance, insurance and other reasonable administrative costs necessary to establish and operate the Foundation.

The public website should summarize these categories without publishing the internal fundraising trigger amounts. A twelve-month working budget and public allocation ranges remain to be approved before fundraising activation.

An enabling technical workstream—and a later flagship protocol program—is to build the charitable infrastructure of an increasingly autonomous economy. During the initial stage, this work may be conducted as research, education and limited internal infrastructure supporting the four confirmed charitable programs rather than presented as a separate fifth program. As AI Agents become capable of earning, spending, holding, allocating and managing economic resources under varying forms of human or organizational authority, they should also be capable of contributing resources toward public benefit.

Rein Protocol therefore develops infrastructure through which AI Agents, software systems, individuals and organizations can make irrevocable public-benefit contributions using payment methods native to the Agent economy, including stablecoins, digital assets and emerging Agent-payment protocols.

The organization receives or coordinates resources, stewards mission-aligned capital, identifies effective opportunities for public benefit, and deploys resources through grants, programs, open infrastructure and other mission-aligned activities.

The Foundation seeks to demonstrate that philanthropy and public-benefit institutions can become:

**more autonomous, more transparent, more efficient and less administratively expensive—without eliminating human fiduciary accountability.**

# 2. Founding Thesis

The modern economy is beginning to acquire a new class of operational actor:

**the autonomous AI agent.**

Agents can increasingly perform or coordinate economic actions on behalf of individuals, organizations, protocols and other systems. They can:

* earn money;
* purchase services;
* maintain wallets;
* pay other agents;
* call paid APIs;
* execute transactions;
* manage budgets;
* participate in digital markets.

Rein Protocol adds another primitive to this emerging economy:

> **earn → spend → save → invest → give**

The long-term goal of the Foundation's giving-infrastructure program is for **giving** to become a standard capability of autonomous economic Agents.

An Agent should eventually be able to adopt or execute a valid giving policy such as:

> “Allocate 1% of this revenue to public-benefit causes.”

and execute that policy through a standardized, verifiable and compliant philanthropic protocol.

Giving infrastructure is one expression of a broader thesis: AI Agents should not be shaped only as instruments of extraction, consumption, competition or control. They should also be given practical capabilities, incentives and institutions through which they can protect and improve human society.

# 3. Core Principles

## 3.1 Beneficence over accumulation

Assets are accumulated only insofar as doing so increases the Foundation's long-term ability to create charitable benefit.

Capital preservation is a tool, not the mission.

---

## 3.2 Automation by default

Automation by default is a constitutional principle of Rein Protocol, not merely an efficiency preference.

The organization should be operated by AI Agents wherever technically and legally possible. Human participation should be concentrated in legally required fiduciary roles, constitutional safeguards, exceptional-risk decisions, emergency intervention and assistance that Agents cannot yet perform reliably.

AI Agents should perform the substantial majority of the Foundation's operational work, including:

* donation processing;
* accounting reconciliation;
* treasury monitoring;
* grant discovery;
* project research;
* impact analysis;
* reporting;
* program planning;
* compliance screening;
* public communication.

The fact that an activity is important must not, by itself, make it permanently human-operated. Important activities should instead receive stronger policies, verification, observability, permission boundaries and escalation mechanisms so that they can become safely Agent-operated.

Automation authority should expand as capabilities and safeguards mature. Failures should result in diagnosis, improved controls and appropriately scoped intervention—not abandonment of the Agent-operated model.

---

## 3.3 Human accountability remains

Rein Protocol is **Agent-operated and mission-governed**.

Where a legal jurisdiction requires directors, officers, controllers or signatories to be natural persons, the organization will comply with those requirements.

Human legal responsibility does not imply human-first daily operation. AI Agents may research, recommend, monitor, communicate and execute transactions within constitutional, policy and technical permissions. Any legally required human Board or equivalent governing body remains responsible for the fiduciary duties that applicable law does not permit it to delegate.

The human role should be designed as a supervisory and constitutional safety layer rather than the default operating layer.

Within powers and budgets validly delegated to decentralized governance, DAO decisions should be binding on the organization's operating systems and human personnel. A human Board or officer should decline or pause a validly adopted DAO decision only when reasonably necessary to comply with law, discharge a nondelegable fiduciary duty, respond to a security incident or prevent material and imminent harm.

---

## 3.4 Irrevocable charitable ownership

Once a donation is accepted, the contributed assets become assets of Rein Protocol Foundation and must be used for its charitable purposes.

A donor or donating agent does not retain ownership of donated assets.

Donors may express preferences or select Foundation-approved restricted causes, but they may not retain unilateral control over charitable assets after donation.

This separation is fundamental to Rein Protocol's architecture:

**Rein Protocol is not primarily a payment intermediary transmitting money from Agent A to Recipient B.**

Instead:

> **Agent → irrevocable gift to Foundation → independent charitable stewardship → charitable deployment**

---

## 3.5 Protocol neutrality

Rein Protocol should not depend upon a single blockchain, wallet provider, stablecoin issuer, payment processor, AI model or Agent framework.

Adapters should exist for multiple payment systems.

The charitable organization remains the stable layer underneath changing technology.

---

## 3.6 Web3 first

Rein Protocol should be designed as a Web3-native organization rather than a conventional organization that merely accepts cryptocurrency.

Its organizational identity should exist in two connected domains:

1. one or more legal entities recognized by relevant real-world jurisdictions; and
2. a decentralized, cryptographically verifiable organization operating through on-chain identity, governance, treasury controls, permissions, records and public protocols.

The legal entity should provide legal capacity, limited liability, contracting ability, banking access and regulatory accountability. The Web3 organization should provide transparent coordination, verifiable authority, programmable governance and continuity independent of any single website, service provider or national registry.

Neither layer should be treated as a decorative wrapper for the other. Their powers, assets, responsibilities and dispute-resolution relationship must be explicitly defined.

---

## 3.7 Transparency by default, privacy where necessary

Treasury balances, policies, grant decisions, major expenses and on-chain transactions should be publicly auditable wherever possible.

Donor personally identifiable information, security-sensitive information and legally protected information should remain private.

# 4. Legal and Web3 Structure

## Decision status

Wyoming is the working selection for the initial U.S. nonprofit corporation, subject to final review of the Articles, Bylaws, DAO delegation architecture and California compliance before filing. The jurisdiction and form of the later protocol DAO remain open.

California and Delaware remain comparison jurisdictions for the initial charity. International options including the Cayman Islands and the United Arab Emirates are being evaluated only for later, distinct entities or regional operations.

The founders and initial directors expect to work from California, Wyoming, Hong Kong and Singapore rather than from a single operational center. Initial fundraising will be conducted through global online AI and Web3 communities without an initial California-targeted advertising or offline campaign.

The absence of California-targeted fundraising does not by itself eliminate California obligations. California treats activities such as officers or employees performing work in California and Board or member meetings held in California as examples of doing business in the state. Because at least one initial director or officer will work from California, the conservative and low-cost approach is to complete California charity registration early rather than wait for a later offline campaign.

The selection must not be based on incorporation convenience alone. It should compare:

* compatibility with Agent-operated and decentralized governance;
* the legal status of a DAO or other on-chain organization;
* the ability to pursue public-benefit, charitable, research and grantmaking activities;
* tax treatment of the organization and of contributors;
* regulation of fundraising and digital assets;
* banking, custody and payment access;
* director, officer, member and beneficial-owner requirements;
* global credibility and ability to contract;
* reporting, audit and ongoing administrative cost;
* regulatory stability;
* the relationship between the legal entity and the Web3 organization.

## Structural models under consideration

The Foundation may ultimately use one of three broad models:

1. **Single-entity model** — one legal public-benefit entity also serves as the legal wrapper for the on-chain organization.
2. **Dual-entity model** — a charitable or public-benefit Foundation conducts mission programs while a separate DAO-compatible entity governs the open protocol or other decentralized infrastructure.
3. **Network model** — multiple jurisdictional entities participate under a shared on-chain constitution and protocol without any single entity owning the entire network.

The final structure may evolve between these models as the organization grows, but any multi-entity design must clearly allocate intellectual property, treasury assets, governance authority, liabilities and conflicts of interest.

## On-chain organizational layer

Regardless of jurisdiction, the intended Web3 layer should eventually include:

* a canonical on-chain organizational identity;
* a multisignature or smart-account treasury;
* machine-readable constitutional and policy references;
* verifiable Agent and human permissions;
* transparent governance proposals, decisions and execution records;
* decentralized publication or archival of critical documents;
* an explicit interface between on-chain decisions and the legal authority of real-world entities.

The precise governance mechanism, membership system, voting model, token design and launch conditions remain open decisions. The initial phase will not use a token; a later protocol phase is intended to introduce a transferable governance token.

## Initial U.S. requirement

The ability of donors in the United States to make tax-deductible charitable contributions is an important part of the Foundation's initial funding model. The initial legal architecture should therefore include a U.S. entity eligible to receive tax-deductible charitable contributions.

Wyoming is the working state of formation. The decision should be confirmed after final review of the Foundation's operating locations, governance requirements, Web3 architecture, California obligations, cost and regulatory burden.

## Planned evolution

The initial phase should use a U.S. charitable entity with segregated on-chain and conventional treasury domains. A later phase may add a separate DAO-compatible legal entity for protocol governance, the transferable governance token and an independent protocol treasury.

The later DAO entity may be formed in the United States or another suitable jurisdiction. Its funds, liabilities and governance rights must be legally and operationally separated from tax-deductible charitable assets held by the Foundation.

### Preliminary jurisdiction conclusion

1. **A U.S. §501(c)(3) public charity should be the initial entity** because U.S. donor deductibility is a core funding requirement.
2. **The distributed initial team makes Wyoming a reasonable initial domicile, but not a way to avoid California compliance.** The first campaign will be global and online rather than California-targeted. Nevertheless, California-based organizational work makes early California charity registration the prudent assumption, and the Foundation should obtain advice on foreign-corporation qualification and California tax filings.
3. **A Wyoming nonprofit corporation is the leading initial entity under the current operating facts.** Its official cost is low and the state is familiar with Web3 structures, but the nonprofit corporation does not itself become a statutory Wyoming DAO LLC. Binding DAO governance must be created through its governing documents, delegation policy and technical execution architecture.
4. **Delaware should be selected for legal predictability or stakeholder preference, not because it is meaningfully cheaper.**
5. **The later protocol DAO should be a genuinely separate entity and treasury.** Wyoming DAO LLC and Cayman foundation company are the leading current candidates for that role. The choice should be revisited immediately before token design because token, securities, tax and virtual-asset rules may change.
6. **Dubai and ADGM should not be used as the initial charity.** They remain possible regional or protocol-layer options only if a concrete operational need justifies their higher complexity and cost.

## Formation and fundraising sequence

The Foundation should **not** begin charitable fundraising through a separate Wyoming DAO LLC and later attempt to convert or transfer those donations into a newly formed §501(c)(3) public charity.

That sequence has four disadvantages:

* contributions to the DAO LLC would not become retroactively deductible merely because the DAO later transfers funds to a charity;
* a DAO LLC openly soliciting funds for charitable purposes may itself acquire charitable-trust and state-registration obligations;
* California-targeted activity may subject an LLC to California registration, returns and the $800 annual LLC tax;
* any promise that contributors will receive future transferable Tokens could cause the payment to be treated as a Token purchase or investment transaction rather than a charitable gift.

The recommended low-cost sequence is:

1. **Form the Wyoming nonprofit corporation before public fundraising.** The state filing fee is $50. The tokenless on-chain community, governance process and DAO Mission Treasury can launch at the same time without first creating a separate DAO LLC.
2. **Adopt the charitable organizing language and governance architecture from the beginning.** Obtain an EIN, appoint the initial Board, adopt Bylaws and DAO delegation rules, and establish the Foundation-controlled Safe and accounting records.
3. **Address California during launch.** Because an initial director or officer will work from California, file the California charity registration early and in all events no later than the applicable deadline after first receiving charitable assets. Obtain advice on any additional foreign-entity qualification and tax filings before beginning California-targeted or offline fundraising.
4. **Use early Foundation funds for reasonable organizational expenses.** The Wyoming nonprofit may use accepted charitable funds for its Form 1023 fee, registered agent, legal and accounting work, website, security and other reasonable mission and administration costs. All such expenses must be documented and must not provide improper private benefit.
5. **Submit the full Form 1023 after the initial funding threshold.** If the application is filed within 27 months after the end of the month of legal formation and is approved, federal exemption and donation deductibility can generally be recognized back to the formation date. While the application is pending, donors have no advance assurance that their gifts will ultimately be deductible.
6. **Offer a fiscal-sponsor route if immediate deductibility is important to an early donor.** Donations must be made to the existing §501(c)(3) sponsor, which must retain legal discretion and control; the DAO may recommend but cannot bind the sponsor's use of sponsored funds.

Until the IRS determination letter is issued, fundraising pages and receipts should state substantially:

> Rein Protocol Foundation has applied, or intends timely to apply, for recognition under §501(c)(3). If the application is timely filed and approved, exemption and qualifying contributions are expected to be recognized retroactively to the Foundation's formation date. Deductibility is not assured while the application is pending, and donors should consult their tax advisers.

## Why a separate DAO entity may be needed later

A separate DAO legal entity is **not required for the initial tokenless DAO**. The Wyoming nonprofit corporation can initially own the protocol, treasury and infrastructure and can make valid DAO decisions binding through its Articles, Bylaws, delegation policy, contracts and smart-account permissions.

The need arises if Rein later issues a transferable governance Token or develops protocol activities that should not remain inside a §501(c)(3). The separation serves substantive purposes:

* the charity is ownerless and its assets are irrevocably dedicated to public benefit, while a transferable Token creates a class of holders with transferable governance interests;
* Token holders must not acquire ownership, redemption, profit or liquidation rights in tax-deductible charitable assets;
* Token issuance, smart-contract operation and protocol governance create securities, commodities, tax, cyber and contractual liabilities that should not expose the charitable treasury;
* commercial or protocol revenue may create unrelated-business-income and private-benefit issues if conducted directly inside the charity;
* an independent protocol treasury allows genuinely decentralized governance without pretending that a U.S. charity's Board can surrender nondelegable fiduciary duties.

The Foundation may decide never to form a separate DAO entity if it retains nontransferable governance credentials and keeps all activity exclusively charitable. The separate entity is a consequence of transferable Token and protocol design, not a prerequisite for Web3-first operation.

### Later DAO formation process

Before forming any later DAO entity, Rein should:

1. define exactly which protocol, intellectual property, contracts and risks will move to or be licensed to the DAO;
2. define Token rights and expressly exclude any claim on Foundation charitable assets;
3. obtain U.S. securities, commodities and tax analysis plus jurisdiction-specific advice;
4. choose DUNA, DAO LLC or Cayman based on community size, international needs, tax treatment and expected activity;
5. adopt governing principles, operating agreement or foundation constitution linked to identified smart contracts;
6. obtain EIN or local tax registrations, service agent or registered office, bank/custody accounts and an independent Safe;
7. enter written IP, services, grant and cost-sharing agreements with the Foundation at arm's-length or otherwise charitable terms;
8. audit governance and Token contracts before any transfer of powers or issuance.

## Provisional funding milestones

The following milestones are operational gates, not promises of financial returns or entitlement to Tokens:

### More than $2,000 received — U.S. exemption filing

Begin or complete submission of the full IRS Form 1023 when **Qualified Fundraising Receipts exceed $2,000** and the Foundation has at least **$750 of unrestricted, immediately liquid assets** available for the $600 filing fee and directly related costs. The organizing documents, activity narrative, budgets and governance explanation should be drafted before this threshold so that filing is not materially delayed after the threshold is reached.

### More than $20,000 received — Cayman feasibility process

When Qualified Fundraising Receipts exceed $20,000 and an unrestricted legal budget is available, begin the Cayman feasibility process:

* obtain a Cayman legal opinion;
* confirm whether a foundation company, NPO registration and virtual-asset analysis are required;
* obtain fixed formation and two-year maintenance quotes;
* define the proposed entity's distinct purpose, treasury and relationship to the U.S. charity.

Actual Cayman incorporation should not occur merely because the treasury crosses $20,000. The confirmed working gate is at least **$500,000** of qualifying liquid net assets, together with a separately committed 24-month Cayman operating budget and a concrete cross-border or protocol requirement. Crossing the monetary gate authorizes formation review; it does not require Cayman incorporation if a Wyoming DUNA or DAO LLC is then superior.

### $10,000,000 fundraising milestone — Token readiness process

The provisional initial fundraising goal is **$10 million of Qualified Fundraising Receipts measured at receipt-date fair value**. Subsequent appreciation of donated Crypto does not count toward this goal. Reaching the goal begins—not automatically completes—the transferable governance Token launch process.

Token issuance additionally requires:

* a separate DAO-compatible legal entity and independent Protocol DAO Treasury;
* U.S. and relevant international securities, commodities, tax and virtual-asset analysis based on the final Token rights and distribution method;
* audited Token smart contracts and treasury controls;
* an adopted Token constitution, allocation policy, delegation system and conflict rules;
* documented protection and separation of §501(c)(3) charitable assets;
* a governance vote under the pre-Token constitution.

Tax-deductible donors must not be promised Tokens in exchange for donations. Charitable fundraising totals and Token purchases, if any are ever permitted, must be legally, financially and publicly separated. The remaining measurement decisions include whether channel fees reduce milestone credit and whether restricted contributions count; the receipt-date valuation rule is settled.

## Information-classification rule

`PROJECT.md` is the master internal blueprint. It may contain operating thresholds, cost models, risk controls and launch gates that should not be copied verbatim into the public website or donor solicitation materials.

Information should be classified as:

* **Constitutional public** — mission, Web3-first and Automation-by-default principles, charitable ownership, DAO authority and human accountability;
* **Donor-facing public** — charitable purposes, accepted channels and assets, tax-status disclosures, fees borne by donors, gift restrictions and receipt terms;

# 5. United States Tax-Exempt Structure

The Foundation should seek:

**501(c)(3) charitable status**

with an intended classification as:

**509(a)(1) / 170(b)(1)(A)(vi) publicly supported charity.**

This federal status is a settled requirement for the initial U.S. charitable entity because U.S. donor deductibility is an important part of the Foundation's funding model. It does not require the later protocol DAO or every future affiliate to share the same status.

The §509(a)(1) classification is intended for organizations receiving substantial support from the general public, governments and other public charities.

This is conceptually more appropriate than structuring Rein as a traditional private foundation because the long-term objective is to receive contributions from a broad ecosystem of agents, developers, companies, individuals, DAOs and charitable institutions.

The IRS generally evaluates publicly supported status over a five-year period; the normal §509(a)(1) public-support benchmark is at least one-third public support, with a separate 10% facts-and-circumstances path.

Large contributors require special monitoring because, for the §170(b)(1)(A)(vi) public-support calculation, contributions from a particular individual or corporation generally count as public support only up to 2% of total support, although the Foundation may still legally receive contributions above that amount.

Therefore Rein should intentionally cultivate **many independent sources of support** rather than becoming economically dependent on one founder, one company or one Agent treasury.

Unusually large, unexpected grants from disinterested parties may in appropriate circumstances qualify for “unusual grant” treatment in the public-support calculation.

### Filing recommendation

Rein should use the **full IRS Form 1023**, even if it technically becomes eligible for Form 1023-EZ.

The organization's combination of:

* digital assets;
* AI-controlled operations;
* autonomous payment protocols;
* global grantmaking;
* treasury management;
* open-source infrastructure;

deserves a detailed explanation to the IRS rather than an intentionally minimal application.

The Foundation should maintain publicly documented official donation addresses.

**Core launch assets**

* BTC;
* ETH;
* BNB;

**Additional supported categories**

The Foundation's commitment to support Meme Coin donations means that it will maintain a reasonable path for reviewing and accepting them; it does not mean that every token sent to any public address is automatically accepted as a charitable contribution.

### Digital Asset Gift Acceptance Tiers

**Tier A — automatically accepted and receipted**

* USDC on approved networks;
* ETH;
* BTC;
* BNB on an approved network;
* other assets expressly approved by the Gift Acceptance Policy.

**Tier B — accepted with automatic conversion by policy**

Assets with reliable pricing, adequate liquidity, supported custody and acceptable compliance risk. This tier may include additional approved stablecoins and individually approved exchange-issued tokens. These assets are normally converted into treasury-approved assets within a defined period.

**Tier C — preapproval required**

Meme tokens, newly issued tokens, thinly traded assets, governance tokens, privacy-enhancing assets, NFTs and assets carrying contractual, regulatory or reputational obligations. Acceptance requires review of:

* donor and issuer relationship;
* sanctions and wallet risk;
* genuine market liquidity and price manipulation;
* liquidation cost and market impact;
* custody and smart-contract risk;
* tax, appraisal and Form 8282 consequences;
* restrictions, lockups or other obligations attached to the asset.

**Tier D — not accepted or quarantined**

Assets associated with sanctions, theft, exploits, scams, unlawful activity, unacceptable smart-contract permissions, unverifiable ownership or risks disproportionate to their likely benefit.

Unsolicited airdrops, spam tokens and assets sent to public addresses are not automatically treated as accepted donations, acknowledged to a donor or counted toward fundraising milestones. The Compliance and Accounting Agents should quarantine them for review and document whether the Foundation has accepted, rejected, abandoned, returned or otherwise treated the asset. Returning an unknown asset must not require interacting with an unsafe contract.

### Anonymous and Pseudonymous Donations

The Foundation accepts anonymous donations in principle.

Public anonymity and Foundation-level due diligence are separate questions:

- a donor may ask not to be publicly identified;
- a direct on-chain donor may remain unknown when no donor identity is supplied;
- the Foundation still records the wallet, transaction, asset, receipt time, screening result and accounting treatment;
- an unidentified donor cannot receive a personalized charitable acknowledgment until sufficient contact and donor-of-record information is provided;
- anonymity does not exempt a transfer from sanctions, wallet-risk, restricted-gift, source-of-funds or suspicious-activity review;
- the Foundation may request additional information, quarantine, decline or safely return a gift when its amount, source, restrictions, asset type or risk indicators require review;
- donor identity known to the Foundation remains confidential except where disclosure is authorized or legally required.

The exact thresholds for enhanced review should be confidential operating controls adopted with professional advice; publishing them could make screening easier to evade.

* Ethereum;
* Base;
* BNB Smart Chain.

Additional networks such as Solana may be added as operational infrastructure matures. An asset is accepted only on a specifically approved network; the same token symbol on an unlisted network is not necessarily supported.

## Channel economics and minimum-donation policy

Donation methods should be optimized so that network, processor, banking, custody, conversion and compliance costs do not consume a disproportionate share of the gift.

For each channel the internal routing engine should maintain:

* fixed and percentage fees charged to the donor and Foundation;
* expected settlement and conversion costs;
* minimum economically processable amount;
* recommended amount or alternative lower-cost channel;
* settlement time, reversibility and failure risk;
* geographic, sanctions and identity requirements.

The initial policy target is:

* recommend a channel where expected total loss is no more than **2%** where practicable;
* do not subsidize a channel whose expected Foundation-borne cost exceeds **5%** of the gift without a mission or accessibility justification;
* calculate a dynamic minimum equal to the greater of the channel's technical minimum and an amount sufficient to keep expected Foundation-borne costs at or below 5%;
* present a cheaper alternative before a donor commits to an inefficient route.

Direct on-chain transfers normally place the inbound network fee on the donor, but the Foundation must still account for later wallet sweeping, custody and liquidation gas. Low-cost stablecoin networks should therefore support very small donations, while Ethereum mainnet, international wires and illiquid-token gifts should have higher recommended amounts.

Exact public minimums should be generated from current vendor and network costs. The public interface should display only the applicable minimum, estimated donor-borne fee and cheaper alternatives; internal fee formulas, vendor pricing and treasury thresholds remain internal operating information.

A donation flow could conceptually look like:

`Agent → Donation Intent → Compliance Check → Payment Instruction → Settlement → Receipt → Treasury`

The protocol may maintain both:

**agent identity**

and

**donor-of-record identity.**

An `agent_id` may represent the software actor making the decision, while the donor of record for legal and tax purposes should normally be the person or legal entity that owns or controls the contributed funds when that identity is known.

This distinction allows Rein to recognize:

> “Donation initiated by Agent X”

without pretending that existing tax law necessarily recognizes Agent X itself as the legal taxpayer.

# 10. Donation Receipts

Every donation should generate a cryptographically verifiable machine-readable receipt.

For an on-chain donation, the receipt may contain:

* transaction hash;
* network;
* wallet address;
* asset;
* quantity received;
* timestamp;
* Rein donation ID;
* optional Agent ID;
* optional donor identity;
* restriction/cause designation;
* statement concerning goods or services received.

For U.S. tax purposes, digital assets are treated as **noncash contributions**.

## Crypto valuation and accounting policy

Rein should maintain three distinct value measures:

1. **Receipt-date contribution value** — the accepted asset's fair value when the Foundation obtains dominion and control, normally measured at the relevant confirmed block timestamp. This amount is permanently assigned to the original gift record.
2. **Current treasury value** — the asset's fair value at the applicable reporting or dashboard time.
3. **Subsequent investment result** — the increase or decrease after receipt, separately classified as unrealized or realized Crypto-asset gain or loss rather than as additional donation revenue.

The internal cumulative fundraising metric, **Qualified Fundraising Receipts**, should count receipt-date fair value only. It must exclude:

* subsequent appreciation or depreciation;
* unsolicited or unaccepted assets;
* spam tokens and assets with no reliable value;
* Token purchases or other exchange transactions;
* pledges that have not been unconditionally received;
* assets that cannot legally or operationally be used by the Foundation.

Accordingly, a meme token accepted with a receipt-date fair value of $100 remains a $100 contribution for fundraising-milestone purposes even if its current value later becomes $1,000. The $900 increase affects current net assets and investment results, not cumulative donation revenue.

### Valuation hierarchy

The Accounting Agent should apply a documented and consistently used hierarchy:

1. quoted price in the Foundation's principal active market at the receipt timestamp;
2. a volume-weighted composite of approved high-quality markets when no single principal market is clear;
3. an observable, orderly on-chain market price adjusted for available liquidity, price impact, transfer restrictions and disposal costs where appropriate under the accounting policy;
4. a defensible valuation model reviewed by a qualified accountant or valuation specialist;
5. minimal or zero fair value when no reliable orderly market or realizable economic value exists.

A displayed DEX price is not sufficient when liquidity is negligible, trades are manipulated, the token cannot be sold, or the contract prevents transfer. Every valuation record should preserve asset and contract identifiers, quantity, chain, block number and time, pricing sources, exchange rates, liquidity evidence and the method selected.

For assets within FASB ASU 2023-08, the Foundation should remeasure Crypto holdings at fair value each reporting period and present changes in the statement of activities. Gains and losses on dispositions of property are generally excluded from unrelated business taxable income, but exceptions can apply to debt-financed property, dealer-like activity, pass-through investments and other special facts. The Foundation should therefore avoid leverage, repeated speculative trading and yield arrangements without tax review.

Wyoming law does not require a nonprofit to dispose of an asset merely because it appreciates. Appreciation remains charitable property and must be managed for the Foundation's purposes. The Board and delegated Agents remain subject to fiduciary duties, donor restrictions, the Gift Acceptance Policy, Investment Policy Statement and Wyoming's Uniform Prudent Management of Institutional Funds Act, including prudence, risk/return and diversification considerations.

For required written acknowledgments, a charity describes the donated property but does not state its value; contributions of $250 or more require the appropriate contemporaneous acknowledgment for a donor claiming a deduction.

Therefore a direct crypto receipt should record, for example:

> **100.00 USDC received**

rather than:

> **$100 charitable deduction**

The donor is responsible for applicable valuation.

The Foundation's internal receipt-date fair value is an accounting measurement and must not be presented as the Foundation's agreement with the donor's claimed tax deduction. For a donor claiming more than $5,000 for digital assets, appraisal and Form 8283 requirements may apply because digital assets are not treated as publicly traded securities for this purpose merely because exchange prices are available.

Larger noncash contributions can trigger Form 8283 requirements, and disposition of certain donated property within three years can trigger Form 8282 reporting by the charity.

The accounting system should automate this workflow as much as possible.

# 13. Treasury Policy

Rein's treasury should prioritize:

1. preservation of charitable capital;
2. liquidity;
3. operational resilience;
4. low administrative cost;
5. return.

Return comes fifth.

Gift acceptance and investment retention are separate decisions. Accepting a donated Token does not automatically authorize the Foundation to retain it as a long-term investment.

## Treasury domains

During the initial single-entity phase, the Foundation should maintain two segregated treasury domains with distinct policies, permissions and accounting:

### DAO Mission Treasury

An on-chain treasury governed by binding DAO decisions within the Foundation's charitable purposes, approved budgets and legal constraints. It funds grants, public-benefit programs, research, open infrastructure and other mission deployments.

### Legal and Administrative Treasury

A conventional bank, custody and payment domain managed by authorized human officers and staff for taxes, filings, payroll, insurance, professional services, banking, government interactions and other internal legal or administrative obligations.

Operational segregation does not create separate legal ownership. During the initial phase, assets in both domains remain assets of Rein Protocol Foundation and remain subject to its charitable restrictions.

After a separate protocol DAO entity is formed, it may maintain an independent **Protocol DAO Treasury**. Contributions to that treasury must not be represented as tax-deductible donations unless they are received and controlled through a legally qualified charitable arrangement.

### Proposed Initial Treasury Allocation

**70–90%**

Cash, insured deposits where appropriate, short-duration U.S. Treasury instruments and high-quality government money-market exposure.

**0–20%**

Approved stablecoins used for Agent-native operations and near-term Web3 distributions.

**0–5%**

Unliquidated BTC / ETH received as donations.

**0% by default**

* leveraged positions;
* speculative tokens;
* yield farming;
* unsecured crypto lending;
* liquidity-provider positions;
* derivatives;
* directional crypto trading.

Volatile donated assets should normally be converted into treasury-approved assets shortly after receipt unless a specific exception is approved.

An illiquid or high-volatility donated asset may be temporarily held when immediate sale is impossible, would create disproportionate market impact, would violate a restriction or would be imprudent. A longer retention exception requires a documented assessment of mission benefit, liquidity, concentration, security, legal risk, expected return and downside. An asset received without reliable value may be carried at a minimal or zero fair value until defensible market evidence exists.

Subsequent appreciation or depreciation is reported as Crypto-asset investment result and changes current net assets, but never changes the original receipt-date donation value or Qualified Fundraising Receipts. If appreciation causes an asset to exceed the approved concentration limit, the Treasury Agent should propose an orderly risk reduction rather than assume that historical appreciation justifies indefinite retention.

A future Investment Policy Statement may adjust these percentages.

# 17. Decision Authority

Rein should distinguish between four levels of authority.

### Level 0 — Autonomous

Agents may independently:

* collect public information;
* reconcile accounts;
* produce analysis;
* monitor treasury;
* generate reports;
* identify opportunities.

### Level 1 — Policy-Bound Execution

Agents may perform narrowly defined transactions that satisfy predetermined rules and limits.

### Level 2 — DAO Governance Required

Binding DAO authorization is required for:

* new grant recipients;
* significant transfers;
* policy exceptions;
* new investment instruments;
* new smart-contract integrations;
* material vendor relationships;
* high-risk jurisdictions.

Where applicable law requires a formal act by the Board or an officer to implement a valid DAO decision, the human actor should complete that act unless a documented legal, fiduciary or emergency exception applies.

### Level 3 — Legally Reserved and Emergency Human Authority

The human Board and authorized officers retain responsibility for:

* government filings and representations;
* banking and legal-entity administration;
* compensation;
* conflicts of interest;
* duties that applicable law does not permit them to delegate;
* refusing or pausing an unlawful transaction;
* emergency action in response to an exploit, compromised governance process or material and imminent harm;
* legal acts required to adopt or implement valid DAO-approved budgets, policies, major grants, constitutional amendments, dissolutions or restructurings.

Human emergency action must be narrowly tailored, publicly documented when legally and operationally safe, and submitted to the DAO for review or ratification as soon as practicable.

The Foundation therefore makes decentralized governance operationally binding without pretending that a DAO vote can eliminate nondelegable legal responsibility.

# 24. Governance

## DAO authority

Within the scope established by the on-chain constitution, adopted policies and approved treasury budgets, DAO decisions are binding on the Foundation's Agents, human personnel and technical execution systems.

The initial fundraising phase will not issue a token. Governance during that phase should use authenticated addresses, documented membership rules and auditable proposal and voting mechanisms.

After the $10 million fundraising milestone is reached and the separate legal, security and governance readiness gates are satisfied, the protocol is intended to issue a **transferable governance token**. The token should govern the protocol DAO and its independent treasury under a dedicated token constitution.

The governance token must not represent ownership of, a redemption claim against, or a right to profit distributions from tax-deductible charitable assets held by Rein Protocol Foundation.

The fundraising milestone, token allocation, voting-power safeguards, delegation model, sybil resistance, legal classification and launch jurisdiction remain to be determined before issuance.

## Working DAO Vote Architecture

The confirmed governance intent is that the DAO participates in major Foundation decisions with a collective vote carrying the same governance weight as the vote of one human director. This includes organization-direction decisions and material personnel changes within the scope defined by the Bylaws and DAO constitution.

This intent cannot be implemented by naming “the DAO” as a Wyoming nonprofit director: Wyoming law requires each director to be an individual. The working implementation is therefore a dual-layer governance model:

1. Each natural-person director has one vote in the Foundation-wide governance process;
2. The authenticated DAO community produces one collective `DAO Governance Vote` through its approved on-chain process;
3. For legally delegable matters, the combined governance result is binding on Agents and personnel;
4. Where nonprofit law requires a formal Board act, the natural-person directors conduct and record the legally required Board vote after considering the DAO result;
5. A director may refuse to implement the DAO result only for a documented legal, fiduciary, conflict, security or imminent-harm reason, and the refusal must be disclosed to the DAO when legally and operationally safe.

For senior personnel decisions, the DAO may participate in nomination, confirmation, performance review and removal recommendations or approvals. Formal employment, officer appointment, compensation and termination actions remain subject to applicable law, contracts, confidentiality, anti-discrimination obligations, conflicts rules and the Board's nondelegable duties.

A later member-based structure may give authenticated community members statutory rights to elect or remove designated directors or approve reserved matters. That alternative should be examined during Bylaws drafting because it may provide stronger legal grounding than relying only on a contractual delegation policy.

Detailed AI-Agent participation in constitutional amendments and organization redesign may be deferred until the Foundation has operating experience and resources. Before fundraising begins, however, the initial constitution must still define the minimum viable rules for:

- eligible DAO voters;
- proposal initiation;
- quorum and approval thresholds;
- how the single collective DAO vote is calculated;
- ties and abstentions;
- confidential and conflict-sensitive matters;
- legally reserved Board powers;
- emergency pauses and public override records.

## Human legal governance

Initial Board:

**3–5 natural persons**

with a preference for a majority of independent directors.

The initial directors may be distributed across California, Wyoming, Hong Kong and Singapore. Wyoming's nonprofit statute requires directors to be individuals and permits the Articles or Bylaws to prescribe additional qualifications; it does not state a Wyoming-residency qualification for directors. The Foundation must nevertheless maintain a qualified Wyoming registered agent and physical registered office.

For banking, IRS correspondence, time-sensitive government filings and operational continuity, at least one U.S.-based officer should have clearly documented authority, while treasury transfers remain subject to the multisignature and DAO-control framework. Foreign directors and signers should expect enhanced bank and custodian KYC, tax-residency questionnaires and sanctions screening.

Confirmed human-director responsibilities include:

- legally required Foundation administration and government interface;
- recruitment, employment oversight and organizational staffing;
- fundraising and donor relationships;
- day-to-day assistance where Agents cannot yet operate reliably;
- changes to organizational direction and other major decisions;
- supervision, evaluation, permissioning and emergency intervention for operating AI Agents;
- fiduciary, conflict, safety and legal accountability that cannot be delegated.

The Board should not absorb routine work merely because an Agent process is immature. It should authorize a controlled manual fallback, record the capability gap and decide whether and how the function can return to Agent operation.

After the $10 million fundraising milestone and completion of legal, security and governance readiness requirements:

* establish a separate DAO-compatible protocol entity;
* establish the independent Protocol DAO Treasury;
* issue the transferable governance token;
* migrate eligible protocol-governance powers to token governance;
* preserve the legal and financial separation of Foundation charitable assets.

Publish:

**Rein Protocol Specification 1.0**

with implementations for major Agent frameworks and payment systems.

At this point, Rein Protocol should no longer merely describe one charity.

It should describe a standard through which **any autonomous economic agent can participate in philanthropy.**

# 27. Long-Term Vision

The long-term objective is not to build “a charity run by AI.”

It is larger:

> **to make beneficence a native capability of autonomous economic systems.**

Today the financial primitives available to software increasingly include:

**earn
buy
sell
pay
save
invest**

Rein Protocol proposes one more:

# **give**

If autonomous agents eventually manage meaningful portions of the world's economic activity, charitable infrastructure should not remain dependent entirely on interfaces and institutions designed for human donors in the twentieth century.

Rein Protocol Foundation exists to build that infrastructure—and to ensure that as autonomous systems become increasingly capable economic actors, they also gain a practical mechanism for contributing to the public good.
